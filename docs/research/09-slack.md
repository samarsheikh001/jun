# 09 — Slack: how others do it, and Slack's rules (as of 2026-10-28)

For C-09 (team replies from Slack), T-13 (manifest setup), and later C-03 / C-12 / C-10 (Slack Connect, AI in it, "Add to Slack"). Three passes: competitors' help docs, the source code of two open-source desks, and Slack's developer docs and changelog. Vendor pages are what each vendor says about itself; anything not confirmed is marked *(unverified)*.

## 1. What competitors do

**Most Slack-first products don't let agents answer *website* chats from Slack.** Plain, Pylon, Thena, Front's Slack inbox and Intercom's Slack channel sync conversations that already happen *in Slack* (customer channels, Slack Connect); agents mostly reply from the desk app, which posts into Slack. Replying to a widget chat from a Slack thread is what Crisp and Chatwoot do, and what our C-09 is.

**Patterns most of them share:** one Slack thread per conversation; replies from the desk post as the agent (their own linked profile, or the bot with their name and avatar); status and assignment by buttons on a card that updates in place, or by emoji (✅ done); people matched by email. **The split:** notes are a typed prefix (Chatwoot), a message action (Crisp, Zendesk), or a separate internal thread or channel (Plain, Pylon, Thena). Only Pylon and Front document syncing edits and deletes.

## 2. Open-source code (read, not run)

**Chatwoot** ([repo](https://github.com/chatwoot/chatwoot), `lib/integrations/slack/`, `app/jobs/send_on_slack_job.rb`):
- Each install brings its own Slack app; OAuth keeps only the bot token (`integrations_hooks.access_token`). Scopes include `chat:write.customize`, `users:read.email`, `files:read/write`, `channels:history`, `groups:history`.
- Out: *every* message (customer, agent, note, activity) posts; the first one is top-level and its `ts` is saved as `conversation.identifier`, later ones go in that thread. Agent messages never open a thread. `username: "<name> (Contact|Agent|Bot)"` + `icon_url`. A Redis lock per conversation stops two threads being created at once (PR #7773). Errors like `channel_not_found` / `invalid_auth` disable the hook and ask the admin to reconnect.
- In: Events API; replies only (`thread_ts` required). Own posts dropped because bot posts have no `user`. `note:` / `private:` prefix → private note, everything else → public reply. Slack user → agent by `users.info` email.
- **Pitfalls:** signature checking only arrived after an issue reported the endpoint as open (#15457 → PR #15480) and is skipped with no secret set; no `event_id` dedupe (only message `ts`, added after duplicates from retries, PR #9187); inbound work runs inside the request (can miss the 3 s ack); lookups not scoped to the workspace; reconnecting to a new channel orphans old threads (#5448); edits and deletes ignored; many attachment and link-format bugs (#4639, #7646, #1658, #8614).

**Papercups** ([repo](https://github.com/papercups-io/papercups), archived; `lib/chat_api/slack/`):
- Two modes: "reply" (a thread per conversation, a **Mark as resolved** button on the first post) and "support channel" (every top-level message, or 👀 by anyone, opens a conversation; replies to unknown threads fetch the whole thread).
- Acks at once and works in a background task. Private-note prefix `\\` or `;;`. Unknown Slack users fall back to the assignee or account owner.
- **Pitfalls:** no signature verification at all; no dedupe of any kind (retries duplicate messages); auto-sync noise in shared channels (#753).

## 3. Slack's platform rules

**Rate limits (the open D-13 question).** On 2025-05-29 Slack cut `conversations.history` and `conversations.replies` to 1 request a minute and 15 messages for apps "commercially distributed outside of the Marketplace" ([changelog](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/05/29/rate-limit-changes-for-non-marketplace-apps)). **Internal custom apps keep 50+ a minute and 1,000 messages** ([clarification](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/06/03/rate-limits-clarity)). So an app each company creates in its own workspace (T-13) is unaffected; one shared app installed in other workspaces (a hosted Jun, or C-10) is affected. How Slack decides an app is "internal" isn't documented (presumably public distribution off) *(unverified)*. Slack's two changelogs disagree on whether existing unlisted installs were limited from 2026-03-03 ([legacy changelog](https://api.slack.com/changelog/slack-connect-api-2025)). The terms also call the Marketplace the only channel for commercially distributing apps, including "templated custom apps that connect to your product" ([terms update](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/10/13/api-terms-update)): fine for a free self-hosted desk, a question for a hosted cloud. Marketplace listing now needs 10 active installs ([2026-09-01](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2026/09/01/slack-marketplace-install-requirement)). Our design barely needs history calls anyway: events bring each message.

**Creating the app.** `https://api.slack.com/apps?new_app=1&manifest_json=<urlencoded>` opens Slack's "create app" with the manifest filled in: scopes, event subscription and interactivity URLs, shortcuts ([manifests](https://docs.slack.dev/app-manifests/configuring-apps-with-app-manifests), [reference](https://docs.slack.dev/reference/app-manifest)). `apps.manifest.create` can create it by API, but needs a configuration token the person makes by hand (12 h) ([method](https://docs.slack.dev/reference/methods/apps.manifest.create)).

**Events over HTTP** ([events API](https://docs.slack.dev/apis/events-api), [verifying](https://docs.slack.dev/authentication/verifying-requests-from-slack)):
- Verify `X-Slack-Signature` = `v0=` HMAC-SHA256(signing secret, `v0:{timestamp}:{raw body}`), constant-time, timestamp within 5 minutes; answer `url_verification` with its `challenge`.
- Answer 2xx within 3 seconds; do the work after (`waitUntil` / a Queue). Slack retries 3 times (at once, 1 min, 5 min) with `x-slack-retry-num`; dedupe on `event_id`, which is unique across workspaces. Over 95% failures in an hour can disable the subscription (apps above 1,000 events/hour). Cap: 30,000 events per workspace per app per hour.
- Thread replies: `message.channels` + `channels:history` (private: `message.groups` + `groups:history`). Posting with a person's name and avatar: `chat:write.customize` (Slack's policy: with their permission, in response to their action). Emails: `users:read.email`. Files: `files:read`, download `url_private` with the bot token, only to `files.slack.com` ([files](https://docs.slack.dev/messaging/working-with-files)). Buttons and shortcuts: form-encoded `payload`, 3 s ack, `trigger_id` single-use within 3 s ([interactivity](https://docs.slack.dev/interactivity/handling-user-interaction)). `chat.update` only edits the bot's own messages.
- Limits: keep `text` under 4,000 characters (truncated at 40,000); 50 blocks; a section's text 3,000 ([postMessage](https://docs.slack.dev/reference/methods/chat.postMessage), [blocks](https://docs.slack.dev/reference/block-kit/blocks)). About one message a second per channel; back off on 429 `Retry-After` ([rate limits](https://docs.slack.dev/apis/web-api/rate-limits)). No fixed outbound IPs for Workers, so never set `allowed_ip_address_ranges`.

**Slack Connect** ([docs](https://docs.slack.dev/apis/slack-connect)): the host org's bot receives external members' messages; events carry `is_ext_shared_channel`, and messages `user_team` / `source_team`. Slash commands and shortcuts work only for the installing team; external files need `files.info`; private channel ids can change when shared (`channel_id_changed`). Slack Connect channels need a paid plan on both sides ([help](https://slack.com/help/articles/115004151203-A-guide-to-Slack-Connect)). Free workspaces: 90 days of visible history, at most 10 apps ([help](https://slack.com/help/articles/27204752526611)).

**AI features.** Streaming replies (`chat.startStream` / `appendStream` / `stopStream`, Oct 2025; in channels only as a thread reply, ≤ 10 minutes, 12,000 characters) ([changelog](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/10/7/chat-streaming), [method](https://docs.slack.dev/reference/methods/chat.startStream)). New apps use `agent_view` (2026-06-30); `assistant.threads.*` gives way to `agents.sessions.*`, `assistant_view` ends Feb 2027 ([2026-08-20](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2026/08/20/agent-updates)). Usable later for C-12's AI in Slack Connect threads; not needed for C-09.

## 4. What this means for Jun

- **C-09 is the Crisp / Chatwoot shape**: one Slack thread per chat, opened when it's handed off (or every new chat, the D-13 setting), a card at the top with the brief, debug summary and buttons (Assign to me, Close, Open in desk) that updates in place, the visitor's and AI's messages posted into the thread, and teammates' thread replies sent to the visitor as the desk member matched by email.
- **Keeping notes out of the customer's view is the main design choice.** A typed prefix (Chatwoot) fails silently when someone forgets it; a message action (Crisp) or internal-by-default are safer.
- **Do better than the open-source code**: always verify the signature (no setup without the signing secret), dedupe `event_id` in the workspace hub, ack at once and work in `waitUntil`, scope every lookup to the workspace and channel, keep the bot token in hub DO storage (as tracker and WhatsApp keys), lock per conversation so one chat never gets two threads, and keep old threads working after a channel change.
- **T-13 is confirmed as the right setup**: each company's own app from our manifest keeps the full rate limits; a single shared app (hosted cloud, C-10) runs into both the 2025 limits and the Marketplace terms.
